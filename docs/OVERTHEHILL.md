# Ideas from Over the Hill

What Over the Hill (Funselektor, October 2026) does, read from the fan wiki
[overthehill.app](https://overthehill.app/) (vehicles, upgrades, map, tools, weather, wildlife,
cosmetics, story pages, read 2026-11-25). The wiki says its figures come from the game files; none
were checked in the game. Each section gives what they do, then what it could be in Spår, where
the world is real map data rather than five hand-built 4 × 4 km areas.

Our own limits, from `DESIGN.md` and `QUESTIONS.md`, apply throughout: calm (nothing chases you,
"no clock, no score"), the day moving by itself, sound, and traces that stay. Fuel is set aside,
animals are on hold, mountains are the focus, downloads stay small.

## Over the Hill in short

- Open-world off-road driving, 1960s–80s 4×4s, vans and trucks; solo or co-op for up to 4.
- 5 areas of about 4 × 4 km (3 in Canada, 2 in Algeria), joined by tunnels and ferries.
- A loop of **finding places** (cabins, crates, scenic points), **challenge trails** that give
  vehicles, **recovery jobs** (tow a wreck), and **meteorites** that keep falling and are
  winched out for money ("pebbles").
- Money buys **upgrades** per vehicle from the one mechanic per area. Cosmetics are only found,
  never bought.
- **Tools** in a few slots: repair kit, ground anchor, jack, planks, camp, meteorite radar,
  a bell that clears the weather.
- **Photographing wildlife** (27 species, a few rare and legendary), **weather** per climate,
  aurora, and a light **story** told by the mechanic in his sleep.

## What they do, in numbers

Kept here so we can borrow the scale without going back to the site.

**Map and money.** Per area: 6–10 cabins, 15–21 crates, 2–17 scenic points, 3–5 challenge
trails, 1–7 recovery jobs, about 30–50 possible meteorite spots, one mechanic. First visits pay:
cabin 10, crate 5 plus a cosmetic, scenic point 15, trail 15–40 (and mostly a vehicle), recovery
25 plus a cosmetic, mechanic 10. All the one-off money comes to 3,285 pebbles; a full set of
upgrades costs 675 per vehicle, so you can afford about four. After that, meteorites are the only
income.

**Discovery.** Places stay hidden until you come within 200 m. Cabins always show; the first
visit to one clears 400 m around it and shows some places near it. The game keeps no "found"
list (the wiki added one).

**Challenge trails.** Dashed routes through checkpoints; more than 100–120 m from every
checkpoint and the trail resets. Restarting puts damage and tools back to how they were at the
start. 18 of 23 trails give a vehicle the first time; no vehicle is ever sold.

**Vehicles.** 21, in four classes by weight: Trail 1,600 kg, Wagon 2,000, Workhorse 2,800,
Expedition 3,200. Heavier classes get a stronger winch (25 → 51 kN), less steering lock (38° →
28°), more torque and a lower top speed. Each keeps its own odometer; a new paint comes every
50 km up to 300 km. Traits are small and characterful: a crawler first gear, an extra fuel tank,
a kettle plumbed into the cooling system.

**Upgrades** (bought in order, free to take off and put back):

| Line | Stages | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 2, 3 | more torque, power and top speed |
| Protection | 2, 3 | crash damage × 0.85, × 0.70 |
| Suspension | 2, 3 | +8 cm, +16 cm travel |
| Tyres | 2, 3 | +0.08, +0.16 grip on every surface |
| Tool slots | 2, 3, 4 | carry more tools at once |
| Water resistance | 2, 3 | air intake moved up; floods after 10 s, 12 s under water (8 s stock) |
| Winch | 2, 3, 4 | rope 11.5, 13, 15 m (10 m stock) |

**Damage.** Only crashes. Nothing below 18 km/h; above that it rises by about 1 per m/s, at
most 28.5 of 100 per hit, then 2 s of grace. Up to 70 % you drive as normal; at 100 % top speed
halves and torque drops to 80 %, but you still drive. Repaired at the mechanic, a cabin, a camp
(+25 for everyone) or with a repair kit (also on a friend within 20 m).

**Tools** (all owned from the start, 1 slot at first, up to 4; slots refill at a cabin or the
mechanic):

| Tool | Owned | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Repair kit | 3 | full repair, yours or a friend's within 20 m |
| Portable winch | 3 | a ground anchor anywhere within rope reach |
| Jack | 1 | puts the vehicle back on its wheels, up to 7.5 m away; needs 2 m clear |
| Planks | 3 | a pair of 8 m planks laid up to 10 m away |
| Camp | 1 | skips 12 hours in about 44 s, +25 repair for all; everyone within 15 m |
| Meteorite radar | 3 | points at the nearest landed meteorite |
| Cloud clearing bell | 1 | clears the weather for everyone |

**Meteorites.** 2–3 per area at a time. When one is pulled out, the next falls 1–5 minutes later
at a random spot. Pulling it out needs 8 s of tight rope, from 1 to 4 winches (one per vehicle),
no two ropes more than 120° apart. The sizes rise with the number of players, so four players
must all pull together on the biggest. The money is shared by everyone hooked on or within 10 m.

**Weather and time.** A day is about 73 minutes. Weather changes every 1–4 game hours, picked
from a list of allowed next states; each country has its own mix (Canada 21 kinds, about 66 %
dry; Algeria 12, 90 % dry). Each area keeps its own time and weather in single player; going
through a tunnel or ferry rolls new ones. Aurora: 15 % a night in Canada. Rain soaks the
vehicle over 2 minutes, it dries 40 s after; a snow storm covers the ground over 2 minutes, which
melts in 12 (5 in rain). Wet lowers grip per surface (rock −0.04 … planks −0.10, ice −0.08).

**Wildlife.** 27 species to photograph, never hunt: 11 common, 10 uncommon, 4 rare, 2 legendary
(Sasquatch at night at one spot, a Saharan cheetah). Each has a time of day and weather it likes;
only a few come out in the wet; none in storms. Fixed spawn points that come alive as you drive
near; one that has fled stays away until you leave and come back. First photo pays 10–100.

**Story.** The mechanic, Günther, dozes off every 15 minutes; drive up while he sleeps and he
talks in his sleep: 48 dreams in random order that tell his life (farm, milk van, racing,
coaching, old age), about 12 hours of play to hear them all.

**Cosmetics.** 197 parts in 14 slots (horns, roof racks, lights, bed loads, bumpers, side steps,
bush cables…), found in crates, recovery jobs and trails. Never sold.

## What it could be in Spår

Grouped by what the player does. **Real data** says what the map gives us that a hand-built
game has to place by hand. **Fit** says how it sits with our pillars.

### 1. Finding places: the map fills in as you drive

*Built 2026-11-26: see "The map fills in as you drive" in `QUESTIONS.md`.*

**They:** places hidden until within 200 m; cabins reveal 400 m and a few places nearby.

**Spår:**
- The map starts as bare terrain (shaded relief, lakes, maybe the big roads). Roads and tracks
  you have driven are drawn in; places you have come near get their name and mark. "The map is
  the trail you have driven" is already a pillar; this makes it literal.
- **Summits and viewpoints reveal what you can actually see.** Work out the view from the
  elevation (a line-of-sight test over the height tiles) when you arrive, and uncover the
  places and roads in sight. A summit then opens a whole valley, a viewpoint in a gorge little.
  A hand-built game cannot do this honestly; we have the real terrain.
- **Huts and lodges** play the part of their cabins: they always show, and the first visit
  reveals their surroundings (the hiking trails out of them, the next hut along).
- The drone sees too: what it flies over could count as seen.
- A quiet tally per region in the logbook ("12 of 30 places"), no points.

**Real data:** `tourism=viewpoint`, `natural=peak`, huts, shelters, lift tops (already in
`poi.ts`); line of sight from the height tiles.
**Fit:** good; it rewards curiosity, not speed. Kept per account on the server like the logbook.

### 2. More kinds of places to find

*Built 2026-11-26, without summit registers: see "More kinds of places to find" in
`QUESTIONS.md`.*

**They:** cabins, crates, scenic points; each first visit gives something.

**Spår:** OpenStreetMap is full of small things worth driving to in the mountains, more than we
use now:
- **Summit registers** (toppbok): on many Swedish summits a box holds a book to sign. Arrive and
  sign it; the book shows who else of us has signed and when, with the vehicle. Kept on the
  server, so friends find each other's names. A real tradition, and it gives the multiplayer
  world a memory without anyone online.
- **Survey points** (`man_made=survey_point`, triangulation pillars), **springs**
  (`natural=spring`), **waterfalls** and **rapids** (`waterway=waterfall`, `rapids`),
  **caves** (`natural=cave_entrance`), **fäbodar** (summer farms, `place=isolated_dwelling`
  and old farm buildings), **historic sites** (`historic=*`: boundary stones, old mines,
  memorials, charcoal stacks), **bridges** and **fords**, **dams**, **fire towers**.
- **Lakes**: reach the shore of a named lake (already asked in `QUESTIONS.md`).
- **The highest point you reached in each region**, kept in the logbook.

**Real data:** the tags above; small, can go into the existing places import.
**Fit:** good. Each needs only a small thing to see when you arrive (as the shelters got).

### 3. Trails to drive: generated challenge routes

*Built 2026-11-26 from hiking routes, snowmobile trails, power lines and ascents, without ski runs,
lift lines or river runs, and without the packed winter lane: see "Trails to drive" in
`QUESTIONS.md`.*

**They:** 23 hand-drawn routes through checkpoints; leave the line by 100–120 m and it resets;
the first finish gives a vehicle.

**Spår:** generate them from the data instead:
- **Hiking and snowmobile routes** (`route=hiking`, `route=snowmobile`): the snowmobile routes
  (skoterleder) are wide, marked and made for winter. Snowmobile routes in winter, tracks along
  the hiking routes in summer.
- **Power line corridors** (kraftledningsgator): cleared, straight, over every hill and bog:
  classic off-road ground. We already draw the power lines.
- **Ascents**: the steepest drivable line from a road to a summit, found from the slope map.
  "Ascent of Ottfjället".
- **Ski runs uphill** (already cleared) and **lift lines**.
- **River and lake runs**: follow a stream from a lake to where it joins the river, using the
  fords.
- Checkpoints every few hundred metres; stray too far and it starts over (gentle: no timer
  needed, though a best time could be offered as an option, see `QUESTIONS.md` "Trips").
- What finishing gives: a line in the logbook, a paint or part, maybe a vehicle.

**Real data:** route relations, power lines, slope, piste ways; all small.
**Fit:** good if the reward is a thing, not a score. A best time stays optional.

### 4. Recovery jobs: tow something home

**They:** tow a wreck to a drop-off at the end of a dotted line.

**Spår:**
- A vehicle in trouble somewhere plausible: an old Volvo in a ditch beside a forest road, a
  snowmobile through the ice near a stream inlet, a timber trailer on its side in a clear-cut,
  a hut warden's quad stuck in a bog. Pull it out with the winch and tow it to the nearest
  village, hut or road.
- Fits our multiplayer winching (towing already works, `multiplayer/towing.ts`), and the
  winch-on-a-chain work.
- A **load to carry** (asked in `QUESTIONS.md` "Real figures"): firewood to a shelter, supplies
  to a hut, a boat to a lake. Heavier, lower, sinks deeper. The trucks' real payloads (900 and
  1,580 kg) make this meaningful.

**Real data:** places to start from (ditches beside tracks, bog edges, thin ice near streams,
clear-cuts) and to end at (huts, villages), all things we already know.
**Fit:** good. A job, not a deadline.

### 5. Something that keeps happening: the meteorite loop

**They:** meteorites keep falling; pulling them out takes several winches at once, more the more
players there are. It is the only income after the one-off rewards, and the main co-op reason.

**Spår:** something that renews itself and needs winching:
- **Storm-felled trees across the roads** (stormfällning, as after Gudrun). After a storm in the
  weather cycle, a few trees lie across tracks; winch them off. Bigger trunks need two or three
  vehicles pulling at once, scaled to the players in the region, like their meteorites. The
  storm gives the weather a consequence, and trees we already have.
- Others in the same spirit: a rockfall on a mountain road, a boulder in a ford, an iced-up
  bridge after a cold night, a snowdrift across a pass after a snow storm.
- Optionally a pointer like their radar: the next blocked road shows faintly on the map.

**Real data:** the roads, the trees beside them, the weather.
**Fit:** good; it is work, not a threat. Needs the winch to pull a loose object (trunk, rock),
not only an anchor.

### 6. Tools in a few slots

*Ground anchor built 2026-11-27, always carried, without slots: see "Ground anchor" in
`QUESTIONS.md`.*

**They:** repair kit, ground anchor, jack, planks, camp, radar, bell; few slots, refilled at
cabins.

**Spår:**
- **Ground anchor** (a stake or a sand anchor): matters most above the treeline, where the fjäll
  has nothing to winch to. The real tree line makes this a real problem in our mountains.
- **Planks or a ladder bridge** for ditches and streams: we have real streams and ditches beside
  every forest road.
- **Jack / turn back over**: a recovery that keeps you in the world instead of a reset to a
  start point.
- **Camp**: see the next section.
- A few slots, refilled at huts and shelters, gives those places a use.
- Skip the bell (weather is shared and part of the mood; anyone can already change it).

**Real data:** treeline and streams decide where each tool matters.
**Fit:** good; tools are how you get unstuck without a red screen.

### 7. Camping and the day

*Built 2026-11-26, without tools or repairs (neither exists yet): see "Camp at a shelter" in
`QUESTIONS.md`.*

**They:** the camp tool skips 12 hours and repairs everyone; all players within 15 m.

**Spår:**
- **Stop at a shelter, camp site or hut and rest until dawn** (or dusk): a fire lit, the light
  turning, the chapter card with the place's name. Shelters (vindskydd) are everywhere in our
  data, and we just gave them a fire ring.
- In multiplayer, everyone in the region near the fire agrees, and the shared clock moves on.
- Perhaps the only place where tools refill or damage mends (if damage comes).

**Fit:** very good; it is the "Ljus" pillar made into something you do.

### 8. Damage that never stops you

**They:** crashes only, nothing under 18 km/h, at worst half top speed; repaired at cabins.

**Spår:** an answer to the open question in `QUESTIONS.md` ("Trees that hold"): if a crash
should wreck something, their model is a calm one. You are never stranded, only slower; a bent
bumper and a crooked steering could show. Mend at a hut or with a repair kit, also on a friend.

**Fit:** fine as long as it stays mild. Their protection upgrade only makes sense if damage
exists.

### 9. Vehicles earned, kept and worn

**They:** vehicles come from finishing trails; each keeps its own odometer, and every 50 km gives
a new paint; four classes by weight with matching winch strength.

**Spår:**
- **An odometer per vehicle**, and **patina** that comes with distance instead of paints: dust
  and mud that build up, stone chips, a sun-faded roof. "Spår" on the vehicle itself. Washed
  by rain and lakes.
- Vehicles could be found rather than given: a vehicle parked at a farm or lodge that you get by
  driving to it, or by a trail or recovery job. With five vehicles now, everything stays open
  for the moment; this is for when there are more.
- **Winch strength by weight**: ours could follow theirs (stronger on the heavy trucks).
- **Small characterful traits** like theirs: we have them already (crawl control, air
  suspension, rear steering); keep going this way rather than with plain stat bumps.

### 10. Upgrades without money

**They:** pebbles buy engine, protection, suspension, tyres, tool slots, snorkel and winch
stages.

**Spår:** our pillars say no score, so not money. Two ways:
- **Earned by doing**: a snorkel after your first deep ford, a longer winch rope after a
  recovery job, wider tyres after a bog trail. A garage at a lodge or village fits them.
- **Just choices**: set them in the garage freely, each with a trade-off (bigger tyres: more
  grip and float, slower to turn; a snorkel: deeper wading; a lift: more travel, higher centre
  of gravity). Our physics already turns these into real behaviour (ground pressure, wading
  depth).

What maps well onto what we have: wading depth (snorkel), tyre width and diameter (ground
pressure), winch rope length, suspension travel, tool slots.

### 11. Cosmetics found in the world

**They:** 197 parts in 14 slots, all found, never sold.

**Spår:** roof racks, light bars, jerrycans, a spare wheel, a canoe on the roof, mud flaps, a
horn, a bull bar, a Swedish flag on the antenna, a moose-warning sticker. Found at places
(a horn at a lodge, a canoe at a lake's camp site) or given by trails and jobs. The procedural
body generator already builds from parameters; parts are more parameters.

**Fit:** good; a reason to visit places that does not count anything.

### 12. Weather by climate, and per region

**They:** each country has its own weather mix and a list of allowed next states; each area keeps
its own time and weather; aurora 15 % a night in Canada.

**Spår:**
- **Real climate per region and month** (SMHI normals: rain days, snow days, fog, cloud): the
  Jämtland fjäll in October is mostly grey and wet, Kebnekaise has more snow and aurora.
- **Allowed transitions** (clear → cloudy → rain, not clear → storm) keep changes believable.
- **Aurora by latitude** and cloud: likelier in Kebnekaise than in Järvsö.
- **The vehicle getting wet and drying** (2 minutes to soak, 40 s to dry after) changes the
  look; **snow building up** in a snow storm and melting after.
- Real weather from Open-Meteo is already planned for later; this is the offline version of the
  same idea.

### 13. Wildlife to photograph (on hold)

**They:** 27 species, by time of day and weather, a few rare ones, fixed spawn points that wake
when you come near; first photos pay.

**Spår (when animals come back):** real Swedish mountain fauna, in places that make sense:
reindeer herds on the fjäll (in Sami grazing lands), moose at bog edges at dusk, ptarmigan above
the treeline, a golden eagle over a cliff, a mountain hare turning white in winter, the rare
lynx, wolverine and arctic fox, a bear in the forest in late summer. Their idea of a few spawn
points that only wake when you are near, and a rare one at a single place, keeps it cheap.
**Photo mode** is worth having on its own (see 15).

### 14. A light story

**They:** one character, 48 short dreams, heard by coming back to him.

**Spår:**
- **Guestbooks in the huts and shelters**: short entries from people who passed before (a
  hiker, a reindeer herder, a forester, an old driver of the same Terrängbil), a few lines each,
  found one by one. Together they could tell one person's life along the valley.
- Or a **hut warden** (stugvärd) at a lodge who tells something each visit.
- Written by us, so it is work; even ten entries per region would be felt.

**Fit:** good, as long as it is something to come across, never a quest log.

### 15. Photo mode and postcards

Not a feature of theirs (photos are only for animals) but implied by it.
- A free camera with the time frozen, depth of field, the chapter-card serif for a caption.
- **Postcards**: a picture taken at a viewpoint or summit goes into the logbook with the place's
  name and date. The logbook becomes an album of the drive.

### 16. Going from one region to the next

**They:** tunnels and ferries join areas; going through rolls new weather.

**Spår:** several of our regions are real neighbours (Vålådalen and Åre, Tandådalen–Hundfjället
and Björnrike). Where a road leaves the region towards another we have, a sign at the edge
("Åre 23 km") and a chapter card take you there, arriving on the same road. The real road
network decides the links.

### 17. Co-op that needs everyone

**They:** the meteorites grow with the number of players, so the biggest need all four pulling
together, ropes no more than 120° apart; the reward goes to everyone near.

**Spår:** the same rule for felled trees, stuck trucks and heavy loads (5 and 4): the jobs scale
with how many are in the region. Our winching each other and chains (`MULTIPLAYER.md`) are the
ground for it. A repair kit on a friend, a shared camp and the summit register (2) are more of
the same.

## Only Spår can do these

Ideas that come from real data and have no counterpart in Over the Hill:

- **Bridges with weight limits** (`maxweight`): the 6×6 cannot cross the small wooden bridge;
  find the ford. A puzzle from the map itself.
- **Gates and reindeer fences** (`barrier=gate`, fences across the fjäll): stop, open the gate,
  drive through, close it. Calm, real, and true to the mountains.
- **Winter roads and ice roads** (`seasonal`, `ice_road`): tracks that only exist when the lakes
  are frozen; we already have lake ice and seasons.
- **Track grades** (`tracktype`, `smoothness`, `4wd_only`): already read for surfaces; could
  also mark a "hard track" trail.
- **The real date and latitude**: midnight sun in June at Kebnekaise, a three-hour day in
  December. A drive to see the midnight sun from a summit.
- **Nature reserves and the terrain driving law**: in Sweden, driving off-road on bare ground is
  forbidden. An optional "light footprint" mode that marks reserves and keeps count of the
  traces left there, or simply a sign at the edge, adds a quiet layer of place. A choice, not a
  rule.
- **A route from the real map**: "Drive from Vålådalen to the Helags hut road", across what
  the map says is there.

## Suggested order

Small first, each one playable on its own:

1. **The map that fills in** (1) and **more places** (2): on top of `poi.ts` and the logbook.
2. **Camp at a shelter** (7): uses the new shelters and the shared clock.
3. **Generated trails** (3) from snowmobile routes, power lines and ascents.
4. **Ground anchor and planks** (6), with the treeline and streams making them matter.
5. **Recovery jobs and loads** (4), then **storm-felled trees** (5, 17) for co-op.
6. **Odometer and patina** (9), **found parts** (11), **upgrades as choices** (10).
7. **Summit registers and guestbooks** (2, 14), **postcards** (15).
8. Later: damage (8), climate weather (12), linked regions (16), animals (13).
